# The Constitution of the Purple Movement

*Ratification status: adopted as the founding draft on 25 July 2026; presented to the first network council for ratification. Amendments by open pull request.*

## Purpose

The Purple Movement builds trusted communities where youth energy and practitioner wisdom meet, so that anyone can turn curiosity into capability, capability into contribution, and contribution into opportunity.

## The promise

Every participant should be able to say:

> **"Show up curious, contribute what you learn, and this community will carry you further than credentials alone ever could."**

## Why purple

Red is the energy, passion, and urgency of youth. Blue is the depth, structure, and wisdom of experienced practitioners. Between them sits a gap that neither can close alone. Purple is what happens when they meet as equals in the same room.

## Principles

The movement is, without exception:

1. **Free and open.** Participation is never sold. Knowledge is never held.
2. **Nonviolent, democratic, nonpartisan, inclusive.** We critique systems, never persons.
3. **Contribution-driven.** Standing comes from what you give: proof of work, sessions logged, people lifted. Attendance is welcome; contribution is the currency.
4. **Evidence-seeking.** We state our limitations in the same breath as our ambitions, verify claims before we publish them, and treat every program as a prototype under honest review.
5. **Youth-enabling with safeguarding.** Young people lead early and are protected always.
6. **Anti-capture.** No sponsor steers, no stage is monopolized, no platform becomes a personal promotion vehicle, and no person, including the founder, stands above these rules.
7. **Succession by design.** Every community we build is designed to outgrow its builders. Graduation into self-governance is the goal from day one.

## The federation

The movement is a federation of connected but independent works, not a single organization:

| Pillar | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Beyond Syllabus** | Treats the syllabus as a starting point, not a cage: open tools for deep understanding and better questions |
| **Beyond Borders** | Maps frontier technology to real problems worth building, anywhere |
| **Beyond Gatekeepers** | The Evolve network: monthly, free, practitioner-led communities of practice that incubate and graduate into self-governance |

Allied, independent works include the μLearn Foundation's peer-learning ecosystem, a living prototype whose evidence and whose limitations both inform this movement, and the AI+Compassion philosophy: *intelligence without compassion is dangerous; compassion without intelligence is powerless; the future needs both.*

## The participant journey

Awareness → Belonging → Practice → Contribution → Proof → Opportunity → Leadership → Stewardship.

Every stage owes the participant three things, grounded in Self-Determination Theory: a meaningful choice (autonomy), visible progress (competence), and a named human who knows them (relatedness).

## The community rules

Three rules bind every community that carries the movement's name, and cannot be amended:

1. **Fixed rhythm.** The same day, every month. Consistency is the product.
2. **Free entry.** Always.
3. **No capture.** Sponsors support, never steer.

## Governance

A network council of community leads and allied-work representatives stewards this constitution. Leadership everywhere in the movement is emergent, term-limited, and succession-bound. Anything in this document may be amended by open process, except the purpose, the free-and-open principle, and the three community rules.

## An honest note on evidence

This movement grows from thirteen years of community practice in Kerala, India. That history gives us patterns we trust and numbers we can show. It does not make our model universal, finished, or beyond criticism. We publish our pilots, including the results that disappoint us.

## Fork this

Everything the movement produces is openly licensed. If these words are useful in your city, your campus, or your country: take them, keep the three rules, credit the communities that wrote them, and tell us what you learn.
